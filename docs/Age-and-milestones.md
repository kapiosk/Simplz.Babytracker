# Age and milestones

## The age in the header

Add a **Born on** date for a baby on **Babies** and their age appears beside the date at the top
of every screen. It counts in whatever unit people actually use at that point: days for the first
fortnight, then weeks up to about three months, then months, then years.

It is optional. Without a birth date nothing appears and nothing stops working — the milestones
still record, they just cannot say how old the baby was.

With more than one baby it follows whichever is selected, so switching the chips under the header
switches the age too.

## Milestones

Tap the age to open them. That and the link on **Babies** are the two ways in; there is no sixth
tab, because this is something looked at now and then rather than at every feed.

Three parts:

- **Around now** — the handful from the guide whose range is open, or about to be, and which have
  not been recorded yet. Tap **Record** on one and it asks when.
- **Reached** — what has been recorded, newest first, each with **how old they were on the day it
  happened**. That is the part worth having: *first smile, five weeks old*.
- **The whole guide** — all of them, in the order a baby meets them, with the ones already
  recorded marked.

**+ Something else** records one of your own — slept through, first tooth, anything. Those are not
from the guide and are not deduplicated, so two may say the same thing if two really happened.

The date is asked for rather than assumed, because a first smile is usually written down a few
days after it happened. The day you give is the day it counts as, and the age shown against it
follows from that.

## What the guide is, and what it is not

It follows the NHS's own framing of these, which is consistently that **babies vary enormously**
and that these are wide ranges rather than dates to hit. So every entry carries a range of weeks,
never a single age.

**Nothing is ever shown as late, missed or overdue.** Past the range the app says only that many
babies have by now and plenty are later, which is just as usual. Nothing is counted against the
baby, there is no score, and nothing here is medical advice.

If something is worrying you, your health visitor or GP is the person to ask, and the reviews in
the red book are there for exactly that.
