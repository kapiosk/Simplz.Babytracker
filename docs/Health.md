# Health

The **Health** tab is for when the baby is unwell: temperatures, diarrhoea, and medicines with
every dose given. It is a record to show the GP, 111 or the pharmacist — when it started, how high
it went, what was given and when — not advice about what to do.

## Temperature

**Temperature** opens a sheet for one reading:

- **the figure** in °C — type it, or tap a preset and nudge it by 0.1. It starts empty on purpose,
  so a reading can never be saved without somebody having read a thermometer. Anything below 34 or
  above 43 is refused as a typing slip.
- **Taken** — when, which is often a few minutes before it gets written down. The usual nudges
  are there.
- **Where** — underarm, ear or forehead, if you want to say. The same baby reads differently by
  each, and it is the first thing anybody asks.
- **Notes** — *after Calpol*, *flushed*, *off feeds*.

The latest reading sits at the top of the page with how long ago it was taken.

**38°C or more is marked *high*,** in words. That is the NHS's own line for a high temperature in a
child, and it is all the mark means.

For babies the NHS singles out two cases where it says to **call 111 or your GP urgently**: under
3 months old with a temperature of 38°C or higher, and 3 to 6 months old with 39°C or higher. When
the latest reading is one of those — which needs the birth date to be set on **Babies** — the page
quotes that line under it, with a link to the NHS page it comes from. It is the NHS's advice,
quoted; the app never adds any of its own. See
[NHS: fever in children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/).

## Diarrhoea

**Diarrhoea** is one tap, like a nappy on Track, with an **Undo** in case it was the wrong button.

It is saved as **a poop marked as diarrhoea**, not as something separate, because it is still a
dirty nappy. So it counts in the nappy totals on the report, appears on the chart, and shows as
*Poop · diarrhoea* in every list. The report's poop tile says how many of them were diarrhoea.

Any poop can be marked or unmarked afterwards: open it with the pencil and choose **Normal** or
**Diarrhoea**.

## Medicines

**+ Add** a medicine and copy it from the prescription or the label:

- **Name** and **dose** — *Infant paracetamol*, *2.5 ml*.
- **At least this many hours apart** and **no more than this many in 24 hours** — both optional,
  and only ever what the label says. The app never suggests either.
- **Course ends on** — for something prescribed for so many days. It moves to *Finished* the day
  after.
- **What it is for, who prescribed it.**

Each medicine being given shows its rules, when it was last given, how many doses are in the last
24 hours, and — when the label's own gap or daily limit is still holding — the time those run to:
*next within the label's limits: 16:30*.

**Given** asks before it records anything, because a dose logged twice is the one mistake here
that matters. The sheet has the time (now, unless you change it), and checks it against the doses
already logged, **both before and after** — a dose is often written down later, between two
others:

- closer than the label's gap to another dose, it says so and which dose;
- more than the label's daily limit in any 24 hours, it says how many that would make.

It still lets you record it — the button changes to **Record anyway** — because it might be
written down late, or the doctor may have said otherwise. It only makes sure nobody does it
without noticing.

The dose box can be left empty for the usual dose, or changed for one that was different.

**Finished** puts a medicine away (open it with the pencil). Its doses stay in the history and it
moves to *Finished medicines* at the bottom. **Delete** removes the medicine and every dose of it,
which is rarely what you want.

## The history

Underneath, everything from the last 30 days in one list, newest first — readings, diarrhoea and
doses together, so the order of events reads straight down: *38.6, Calpol, diarrhoea, 38.4*. High
readings are marked. The pencil on any line edits it.

## The read-only password

The doctor password sees everything on the page — the readings, the medicines, every dose — and
can change none of it.
